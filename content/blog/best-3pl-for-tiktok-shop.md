TikTok Shop's order spikes aren't random luck — they trace to specific, identifiable mechanisms: an affiliate creator's video getting picked up by the algorithm, a LIVE shopping event driving concentrated real-time purchases, or a Shop Ads campaign hitting its stride. Understanding which one is driving a given spike matters less for fulfillment than the fact that TikTok Shop tracks handling time and shipping performance as Shop performance metrics regardless of why volume jumped — a spike doesn't earn you a grace period on the same SLA every other order is held to.

## Fulfilled by TikTok vs. Seller-Fulfilled

TikTok Shop offers Fulfilled by TikTok (FBT), where TikTok stores and ships inventory similarly to Amazon FBA, alongside a seller-fulfilled path where the seller (or their 3PL) handles storage and shipping directly. DeliveryGroup supports the seller-fulfilled path, which keeps branding and packaging control with you — genuinely useful for creators and brands whose unboxing experience is itself part of the content loop that drives sales — while still needing to meet the same handling-time standard TikTok Shop applies to every seller.

## Where the Spikes Actually Come From

- **Affiliate creator content.** TikTok Shop's affiliate/creator marketplace lets creators earn commission promoting products in their own videos — when one of those videos gets algorithmic pickup, the resulting spike is tied to that specific video's reach, not your own posting schedule.
- **LIVE shopping events.** Real-time LIVE selling sessions concentrate demand into the exact window the stream is live, producing a sharper, shorter-duration spike than typical organic virality.
- **Shop Ads.** Paid campaigns can produce a more gradual, sustained volume increase rather than a single sharp spike, depending on budget and targeting.

Each pattern stresses fulfillment slightly differently — a LIVE-selling spike needs to be absorbed in hours, while an affiliate video's reach might build and sustain over several days. DeliveryGroup's warehouse capacity is built to flex across both patterns rather than being tuned for only one.

## Handling Time Is Still a Tracked Metric During a Spike

TikTok Shop's Shop performance metrics include handling time and shipping compliance, feeding into your account's standing and eligibility for algorithm-boosted placement the same way any marketplace's seller-performance system does. A viral spike that overwhelms fulfillment and blows through the handling-time window doesn't get a pass just because the volume was unpredictable — which is exactly the scenario a 3PL built for burst capacity is meant to prevent.

## TikTok Shop API and Seller Center Integration

DeliveryGroup connects to TikTok Shop through its Seller Center API for order sync and tracking pushback, so a sudden volume spike routes into the fulfillment queue automatically rather than requiring manual order entry at the exact moment your team is least able to keep up with manual work.

## Inventory Depth Matters More Than It Does on Steadier Channels

Because TikTok Shop demand can shift quickly based on a single piece of content, running lean on inventory carries more risk here than on a channel with predictable, steady demand — a video hitting at the wrong moment against thin stock means missing the sales window entirely, with no way to recapture that specific spike later. This is worth factoring into how much safety stock you keep at the warehouse if TikTok Shop is a meaningful part of your sales mix.
