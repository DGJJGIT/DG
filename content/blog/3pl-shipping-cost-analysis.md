## Most E-Commerce Brands Are Overpaying for Shipping

Here is a hard truth. Most e-commerce brands spend 15-25% more on shipping than they need to. They accept carrier rate cards at face value. They ignore surcharge creep. They never question dimensional weight calculations. And they leave thousands of dollars on the table every single month.

The problem is not that shipping is expensive. Shipping is expensive. The problem is that most brands never take the time to understand exactly what they are paying for. They see a total on an invoice and move on. That lack of visibility is where the waste hides.

A structured shipping cost analysis changes everything. It breaks down every component of your shipping spend. It identifies where you are overpaying. And it gives you a clear roadmap to reduce costs without sacrificing delivery speed or reliability.

## What a Shipping Cost Analysis Actually Includes

A shipping cost analysis is not just comparing rates between UPS and FedEx. It is a deep examination of every factor that drives your shipping costs. Here is what a thorough analysis covers.

**Base rate evaluation.** This is the starting point. What are you paying per package across every service level? How do those rates compare to market benchmarks? Most brands only look at their negotiated rates. A proper analysis compares those rates against what similarly sized shippers are paying.

**Surcharge audit.** Surcharges now represent 25-35% of total shipping costs for many brands. Fuel surcharges, residential delivery surcharges, delivery area surcharges, additional handling fees, and peak season surcharges all add up fast. A cost analysis identifies every surcharge you are paying and flags the ones that can be reduced or eliminated.

**Dimensional weight analysis.** Carriers charge based on the greater of actual weight or dimensional weight. If your packaging is not optimized, you are paying for air. A cost analysis measures the gap between your actual weight and DIM weight charges to quantify the savings available through better packaging.

**Zone distribution mapping.** Shipping zones drive a huge portion of your costs. The farther a package travels, the more it costs. A cost analysis maps where your orders are going and identifies whether your fulfillment location is optimized for your customer base.

**Service level mix review.** Are you shipping everything via express when ground would arrive in the same timeframe? Many brands default to faster service levels out of habit. A cost analysis shows you where slower, cheaper options deliver the same customer experience.

## The Surcharge Problem Nobody Talks About

Surcharges are the silent budget killer. Carriers have added dozens of surcharges over the past five years. Many brands do not even realize they are paying them.

Here are the most common surcharges that inflate shipping costs.

**Fuel surcharges.** These fluctuate weekly and can add 10-15% to your base rate. They are often calculated differently for ground and air shipments.

**Residential delivery surcharges.** If you ship to consumers, you pay this on nearly every package. It typically adds $4-6 per shipment. For a brand shipping 5,000 packages a month, that is $20,000 to $30,000 per month in residential surcharges alone.

**Delivery area surcharges.** Packages going to rural or remote areas cost more. If a meaningful portion of your customers live outside metro areas, this surcharge adds up quickly.

**Additional handling surcharges.** Packages that are oversize, overweight, or irregularly shaped trigger additional handling fees. These can range from $3 to $30 per package depending on the carrier and the issue.

**Peak season surcharges.** During Q4, carriers add temporary surcharges that can increase costs by 15-25%. Planning around peak pricing is essential for brands with seasonal demand.

A shipping cost analysis quantifies every surcharge and identifies which ones you can reduce through operational changes, carrier negotiation, or switching to a 3PL that has already negotiated those surcharges down.

## Dimensional Weight and Why Your Packaging Matters

Dimensional weight, or DIM weight, is how carriers charge for lightweight but bulky packages. The formula is simple. Length times width times height, divided by a DIM factor (usually 139 for domestic shipments).

If your DIM weight is higher than your actual weight, you pay for the DIM weight. This means oversized boxes cost you money even when the product inside is light.

Here is a real example. A brand ships a product that weighs 2 pounds in a box that measures 18 x 14 x 10 inches. The DIM weight calculation is 18 x 14 x 10 divided by 139, which equals 18.1 pounds. That brand is paying for 18 pounds of shipping on a 2-pound product.

Right-sizing your packaging can cut shipping costs by 10-20%. A cost analysis identifies every SKU where DIM weight is inflating your costs and recommends specific box sizes to close the gap.

At Delivery Group, we help clients optimize their packaging as part of our fulfillment process. We stock multiple box sizes and use the smallest box that safely fits each order. This is one of the easiest ways to reduce shipping costs immediately.

## Zone Optimization and Fulfillment Location Strategy

Every shipping zone adds cost. A package shipped from Zone 2 costs significantly less than the same package shipped from Zone 8. Your fulfillment center location determines your zone distribution.

If your warehouse is in Los Angeles and 60% of your customers are on the East Coast, you are paying premium zone rates on most of your shipments. Moving fulfillment closer to your customers reduces zones and reduces costs.

Northern Kentucky is within a two-day ground delivery window of over 60% of the U.S. population. That means more of your packages ship in lower zones, which means lower costs.

A zone optimization analysis maps your order history against fulfillment locations to show you exactly how much you would save by shifting to a more central location.
