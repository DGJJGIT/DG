Poshmark's shipping model has one quirk that changes fulfillment math directly: it uses a fixed, flat-rate prepaid shipping label (a USPS Priority Mail flat-rate box) regardless of what's inside, up to a set weight limit. Ship under that limit and the flat rate covers it cleanly. Go over it, and the seller — not the buyer — eats the cost difference. On resale apparel with real variation in weight (a t-shirt versus a winter coat), that's a genuine per-order economics problem, not a rounding error.

## The Flat-Rate Weight Cap Is a Real Constraint, Not Boilerplate

Because Poshmark's shipping label is priced flat regardless of contents, packing choices that add unnecessary weight — excess tissue paper, a bulkier box than needed, extra packaging material — come directly out of seller margin the moment a shipment crosses the weight threshold. DeliveryGroup weighs and packs Poshmark orders with this cap specifically in mind: minimal added packaging weight so more of your catalog stays comfortably under the flat-rate limit, and a heads-up if a specific listing (a heavy coat, boots, multiple bundled items) is realistically going to exceed it so you can price or list accordingly.

## Bundles Mean Picking Multiple Unique Items Into One Shipment

Poshmark's Bundle feature lets a buyer combine several items from your closet into a single purchase and a single shipment — which means fulfillment isn't always "pick one item, ship it." It's frequently "locate three or four specific, one-of-a-kind pieces scattered across inventory, and consolidate them into one box before the shipping clock matters." This is a meaningfully different pick workflow than a typical single-SKU order, and it's exactly the kind of thing that goes wrong when a fulfillment operation is built only around simple one-item-per-order logic. DeliveryGroup's unique-item tagging (each resale piece gets its own tracked location, the same way it would for a Mercari or eBay one-off) makes multi-item bundle picks accurate instead of a scavenger hunt.

## Posh Parties Create Their Own Kind of Order Burst

Poshmark's live virtual shopping events (Posh Parties) can generate a concentrated burst of sales in a short window, similar in shape to a viral social-media spike even though the mechanism is different. A fulfillment operation that can only handle steady, predictable daily volume struggles the same way during a good Posh Party as it would during any other sudden demand spike. DeliveryGroup's warehouse capacity is built to absorb short bursts without your shipping-time metrics slipping the day after a party performs well.

## Apparel-Specific Handling

Clothing needs different physical handling than most e-commerce categories — items need to arrive wrinkle-free and looking like the listing photos, not like they were stuffed into a poly mailer and crushed in transit. DeliveryGroup supports garment-appropriate packaging (folded with tissue, or on a hanger in a garment bag when appropriate), proper poly bagging for protection from dust and moisture during storage, and organized hanging or shelf storage so apparel doesn't develop new wrinkles sitting in inventory before it sells.

## Condition Documentation for Resale Disputes

As with any resale marketplace, buyers are more sensitive to undisclosed condition issues than they would be with new retail goods. DeliveryGroup can photo-document an item's condition at receiving on request, which is useful evidence if a buyer disputes an item's condition after delivery.
