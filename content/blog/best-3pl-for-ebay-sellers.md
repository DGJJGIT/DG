Most eBay sellers know that slow shipping leads to bad feedback. Fewer realize that eBay's search algorithm, Cassini, factors seller performance metrics — including late-shipment rate and handling time — directly into how often your listings surface in search results. A fulfillment delay doesn't just risk one bad review; it can quietly suppress every listing you have.

## Handling Time Is a Ranking Factor, Not Just a Promise

eBay tracks your actual handling time against what you advertise on each listing, and factors deviations into your seller performance standing. Top Rated Plus status — which comes with a visible badge, priority placement in search, and eligibility for eBay's 30-day money-back guarantee program — requires same-day or one-day handling time and consistently valid tracking uploads. DeliveryGroup's same-day pick-and-pack for orders received before the daily cutoff is built to keep same-day or next-day handling time achievable at real volume, not just on your best days.

## Tracking Upload Timing Matters as Much as Shipping Speed

eBay requires tracking numbers to be uploaded promptly and to be valid and scannable — a tracking number that doesn't update, or uploads late, counts against your seller metrics the same way a genuinely late shipment does. DeliveryGroup pushes tracking numbers back to your eBay listings automatically the moment a label is generated, not after a batch job runs later in the day, so the tracking clock starts when the package actually enters the carrier network.

## eBay Guaranteed Delivery and Carrier-Calculated Estimates

eBay's Guaranteed Delivery program requires accurate handling time and reliable carrier-calculated delivery date estimates — if the estimate eBay shows a buyer is wrong because your actual fulfillment doesn't match your stated handling time, you lose eligibility for the program's search-visibility boost. Because DeliveryGroup ships from Florence, Kentucky — reaching roughly 80% of US addresses in 2–3 days by ground — carrier-calculated estimates tend to be both fast and reliably accurate, which is what the Guaranteed Delivery program actually rewards.

## Item Specifics, Variations, and Multi-Quantity Listings

eBay listings built around item specifics and variations (size, color, style) map to individual SKUs the same way a Shopify or BigCommerce variant does — but eBay sellers running multi-quantity listings (one listing, many identical units) need inventory sync that decrements correctly per sale rather than per listing. DeliveryGroup's eBay integration syncs at the SKU level so a multi-quantity listing's available count updates in real time as units sell, preventing the overselling that happens when a listing's quantity field and your actual warehouse stock drift apart.

## The Global Shipping Program and International Orders

Sellers using eBay's Global Shipping Program (GSP) ship domestically to an eBay-designated shipping center, which then handles international customs and forwarding — meaning your fulfillment partner only needs to get the package to that US-based shipping center reliably and on time, not manage customs paperwork directly. For eBay sellers not using GSP and shipping internationally themselves, DeliveryGroup's DHL partnership covers that instead, with the same rate advantages that apply to domestic orders.

## Returns and eBay's Money-Back Guarantee

eBay's money-back guarantee puts real pressure on return processing speed and accuracy, since delayed or mishandled returns show up in seller performance metrics the same way late shipments do. DeliveryGroup inspects returned items against your stated criteria, restocks sellable inventory, and flags anything damaged — keeping your eBay inventory count and your actual warehouse stock in agreement.
