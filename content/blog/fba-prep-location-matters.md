Most Amazon sellers obsess over product sourcing, listing optimization, and PPC bids. But they overlook the single biggest variable in their cost structure: where their FBA prep center sits on the map.

Your prep center's ZIP code affects every shipment you send to Amazon. It determines how much you pay in inbound shipping. It controls how fast your inventory reaches fulfillment centers. And it directly impacts whether Amazon hits you with placement fees.

If you are still shipping from a coastal warehouse, you are likely overpaying by 20-40% on inbound logistics alone. Let us break down why location matters more than almost any other factor in your FBA prep strategy.

## Inbound Shipping: The Cost Most Sellers Underestimate

Inbound shipping is the cost of moving your products from your prep center to Amazon's fulfillment centers. For most sellers, this is the single largest expense after the cost of goods sold.

Here is the math that matters. Amazon uses a network of fulfillment centers spread across the country. When you send inventory, Amazon decides which FCs receive your products. You pay the freight to get them there.

The farther your prep center is from those FCs, the more you pay. It is that simple.

A seller shipping from Los Angeles to an Amazon FC in Kentucky pays significantly more than a seller shipping from Northern Kentucky to that same FC. The difference is not small. We are talking about dollars per unit on heavy or oversized items.

Over thousands of units per month, that gap becomes tens of thousands of dollars per year.

## Amazon Placement Fees: The Hidden Tax on Bad Location

In 2024, Amazon introduced inbound placement service fees. These fees apply when sellers send inventory to a single location instead of distributing it across Amazon's network.

Here is what this means in practice. Amazon wants your inventory spread across multiple FCs so they can deliver to customers faster. If you send everything to one FC, Amazon has to redistribute it internally. They charge you for that.

The placement fee can range from $0.27 to over $1.58 per unit depending on size and weight. For high-volume sellers, this adds up fast.

A centrally located prep center helps reduce these fees in two ways. First, you are closer to more FCs, so Amazon is more likely to route your shipments to nearby facilities. Second, you can split shipments across multiple regional FCs without paying astronomical freight costs.

When your prep center is in Northern Kentucky, splitting shipments to FCs in Ohio, Indiana, Tennessee, and Kentucky is cheap. The distances are short. The transit times are fast. The fees stay low.

When your prep center is on the coast, splitting shipments means sending freight halfway across the country to multiple locations. That gets expensive quickly.

## What to Look for in an FBA Prep Center Location

If you are evaluating prep centers, here are the location factors that matter most.

Proximity to Amazon FCs is the top priority. Count how many FCs are within a 200-mile radius. The more FCs nearby, the lower your average inbound cost.

Access to major highways matters. Your prep center should sit near interstate highways that connect to Amazon's distribution network. Florence, KY sits at the intersection of I-75 and I-71, two of the busiest freight corridors in the eastern United States.

Airport access is important for express and air freight. Being near a major cargo airport like CVG gives you options when speed matters.

Carrier options affect your rates. A location with multiple carrier terminals means more competition and better rates. Northern Kentucky has terminals for every major carrier in the country.

Labor availability affects your prep center's capacity and reliability. The NKY/Cincinnati metro area has a large workforce experienced in logistics and warehousing.
