Every e-commerce brand starts by packing orders themselves. Maybe it is your garage, your spare bedroom, or a small warehouse you rented. And for a while, it works. You control everything, you know every product, and you can pack orders exactly the way you want.

But at some point, fulfillment starts to hold you back. You spend more time packing boxes than growing your business. You cannot take a vacation without orders piling up. Peak season becomes a nightmare. That is when most brands start thinking about a 3PL.

This guide will help you figure out when it makes sense to switch and what to expect when you do.

## What Is a 3PL?

A 3PL, or third-party logistics provider, is a company that stores your inventory, picks and packs your orders, and ships them to your customers. You send your products to their warehouse. When a customer places an order on your website or marketplace, the 3PL handles everything from that point forward.

Good 3PLs integrate directly with your selling platforms so orders flow automatically. You do not have to manually forward anything. The 3PL picks the items, packs them in your packaging, ships them, and sends tracking information back to your store.

## The Real Cost of In-House Fulfillment

Most brand owners underestimate the true cost of doing fulfillment themselves. They think about the obvious costs like packaging materials and postage. But they miss the hidden costs that add up fast.

**Labor costs.** Your time has value. If you spend 4 hours a day packing orders, that is 4 hours you are not spending on marketing, product development, or customer relationships. If you hire staff, you need to pay wages, benefits, and payroll taxes.

**Space costs.** Warehouse space is not free. Whether you are renting a dedicated space or using part of your home, there is a cost. As your inventory grows, so does the space you need.

**Shipping rates.** Individual sellers pay retail shipping rates. A 3PL with volume discounts can ship the same package for significantly less. DeliveryGroup's direct DHL partnership provides rates that most individual sellers and small 3PLs cannot access.

**Mistakes and delays.** When you are packing hundreds of orders, mistakes happen. Wrong items, missed orders, late shipments. Each mistake costs money in reshipping, refunds, and lost customers.

**Opportunity cost.** This is the biggest hidden cost. Every hour you spend on fulfillment is an hour you are not spending on activities that grow revenue. For most brands, the return on investing that time in marketing or product development far exceeds the cost of a 3PL.

## When to Stay In-House

In-house fulfillment makes sense in some situations:

- **Very low volume.** If you ship fewer than 100 orders per month, the cost of a 3PL may not make financial sense yet.
- **Highly customized orders.** If every order requires unique customization that a 3PL cannot replicate, staying in-house gives you control.
- **You enjoy it.** Some brand owners genuinely enjoy the hands-on fulfillment process and are not looking to scale aggressively.

## Signs You Have Outgrown In-House Fulfillment

Here are the signals that it is time to talk to a 3PL:

- You are spending more than 2 hours a day on fulfillment
- You have missed shipping deadlines or had accuracy issues
- You cannot take time off without orders falling behind
- Peak season (Q4) overwhelms your operation
- Your shipping costs are eating into your margins
- You are turning down wholesale or marketplace opportunities because you cannot handle the volume
- You need to hire dedicated fulfillment staff

If three or more of these describe your situation, a 3PL will almost certainly save you money and free up time for growth.

## What to Look for in a 3PL

Not all 3PLs are created equal. Here is what matters:

**Location.** A centrally located warehouse means faster, cheaper shipping to more of the country. DeliveryGroup's Northern Kentucky location reaches over 80 percent of the US in 2 to 3 days by ground.

**Shipping rates.** Ask about carrier partnerships. DeliveryGroup's DHL direct-inject partnership provides rates most 3PLs cannot match.

**Technology.** The 3PL should integrate with your selling platforms and provide real-time tracking and reporting. You should not have to manually forward orders.

**Scalability.** Can they handle 10x your current volume? A good 3PL grows with you without missing a beat.

**Service.** Do you get a dedicated account manager or a ticketing system? The difference matters when something goes wrong.

**FBA prep.** If you sell on Amazon, look for a 3PL that also handles FBA prep. Running both from one location simplifies everything.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**At what order volume should I switch to a 3PL?**
Most brands benefit from a 3PL at 200 to 500 orders per month. Below that, the math usually favors in-house. Above that, a 3PL almost always saves money and time.

**Will my customers know a 3PL is handling fulfillment?**
No. A good 3PL uses your packaging, your inserts, and your branding. To your customer, the order comes from you.

**Can I still include custom inserts and thank-you cards?**
Yes. DeliveryGroup supports custom packaging, marketing inserts, thank-you cards, and promotional materials in every order.

**What if I am not happy with the 3PL?**
Look for providers with flexible contract terms. DeliveryGroup offers month-to-month arrangements as well as annual contracts.

**How much cheaper is 3PL shipping vs my own rates?**
Most brands save 20 to 40 percent on shipping by using a 3PL's negotiated carrier rates. The exact savings depend on your package sizes and shipping zones.

**Can I keep some fulfillment in-house and outsource the rest?**
Yes. Some brands handle local orders themselves and use a 3PL for nationwide shipping. DeliveryGroup can work alongside your in-house operation.
