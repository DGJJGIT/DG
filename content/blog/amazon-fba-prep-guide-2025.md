Amazon FBA is one of the most powerful tools available to online sellers. You send your products to Amazon's warehouses. They store, pick, pack, and ship orders for you. They handle customer service and returns. It sounds simple.

But getting products into Amazon's warehouses is not simple at all. Amazon has strict requirements for how items must be prepared, labeled, and shipped. Get it wrong and your inventory gets rejected, delayed, or hit with extra fees.

This guide covers everything sellers need to know about FBA prep in 2025. We break down the requirements, the costs, and the strategies that successful sellers use to stay compliant and profitable.

## What Is FBA Prep?

FBA prep is the process of getting your products ready to ship to Amazon's fulfillment centers. It includes everything that happens between receiving your products from the manufacturer and loading them onto a truck bound for Amazon.

The basics include inspecting products, applying FNSKU labels, adding suffocation warnings where required, poly bagging certain items, bundling multi-packs, and boxing everything according to Amazon's specifications.

Every product is different. A hardcover book needs an FNSKU label and maybe a poly bag. A glass bottle needs bubble wrap, a poly bag, and a fragile label. A bundle of three items needs to be sealed together with a special bundle label.

FBA prep can be done by the seller, the manufacturer, or a third-party prep center. Most successful sellers use a dedicated prep center. It saves time, reduces errors, and lets sellers focus on sourcing and marketing.

## 2025 FBA Requirements You Need to Know

Amazon updates its FBA policies regularly. Here are the key requirements for 2025 that every seller must follow.

**FNSKU labeling** is required on every unit. The FNSKU is a unique barcode that ties the product to your seller account. Amazon uses it to track your inventory separately from other sellers with the same product. Every unit needs a scannable FNSKU label in the right position.

**Suffocation warnings** are required on all poly bags with an opening of 5 inches or larger. The warning must be printed on the bag or added as a separate label. It must be in a specific font size. Missing suffocation warnings will get your shipment rejected.

**Poly bagging** is required for products that could be damaged by dust, moisture, or handling. This includes textiles, plush items, items with loose parts, and anything in packaging that is not sealed. The poly bag must be transparent, sealed, and have the suffocation warning.

**Bubble wrapping** is required for fragile items. Glass, ceramics, and items with screens need at least two layers of bubble wrap. The wrapped item must still fit within standard box dimensions.

**Expiration dates** must be visible on the outside of the packaging for perishable items. If the manufacturer date is on the bottom of the product, you need to add a separate label with the expiration date in a visible location.

**Box content accuracy** has become more strictly enforced. Every box you send to Amazon must have contents that match what you declared in your shipping plan. Random spot checks are common. Discrepancies result in fees and can lead to shipping plan restrictions.

## Labeling: FNSKU, Suffocation, and More

Labeling is the most important part of FBA prep. A mislabeled product causes problems at every stage of the process.

The FNSKU label must be placed so it is scannable without opening the product packaging. For most items, this means on the outside of the poly bag if the item is bagged. For unbagged items, it goes directly on the product packaging.

Cover any existing barcodes on the product. If the product has a UPC or EAN barcode, place the FNSKU over it or cover the old barcode with an opaque label. Two scannable barcodes on one product confuse Amazon's system and lead to inventory errors.

FNSKU labels must be printed at high quality. Blurry or smudged barcodes cannot be scanned. Use a thermal label printer for best results. Inkjet labels smear when they get wet. Laser labels can peel off in warm warehouses.

For bundles, the FNSKU goes on the outside of the bundle packaging. Each individual item inside the bundle does not get its own FNSKU. Only the bundle unit gets labeled.

Suffocation warning labels must include the exact language Amazon specifies. The text varies slightly depending on the size of the bag. Using incorrect warning text can result in a rejection even if you have a label present.

## Poly Bagging Best Practices

Poly bagging protects products and keeps them clean. But there are right and wrong ways to bag items for FBA.

Use clear poly bags. Amazon requires transparency so warehouse workers can scan the FNSKU through the bag. Colored or opaque bags are not allowed.

The bag must be sealed. Use self-seal bags for efficiency. Heat sealing works too but takes more time. The seal must be secure enough that the bag does not open during handling.

Bag thickness matters. Amazon recommends at least 1.5 mil thickness for most products. Thinner bags tear during shipping and handling. Heavier items may need thicker bags.

Do not make the bag too large for the product. Excess bag material creates folds that can obscure barcodes. The bag should fit the product with minimal extra space.

For products with sharp edges or corners, add corner protectors before bagging. A sharp corner can puncture the poly bag during transit, defeating the purpose of bagging in the first place.

## Bundling and Multi-Pack Requirements

Bundles and multi-packs are great for increasing average order value. But Amazon has specific rules about how to prep them.

A bundle must be physically packaged together. You cannot just list three separate items as a bundle. They must be in one sealed package with one FNSKU label.

Use shrink wrap, poly bags, or bundling tape to hold bundle components together. The packaging must be secure enough that the bundle cannot come apart during handling. If a bundle comes apart in Amazon's warehouse, the individual items get separated and lost.

Each bundle needs a label that says "Sold as set. Do not separate." This tells Amazon's warehouse team that the items belong together. Without this label, workers may assume the items were accidentally grouped and separate them.

Multi-packs of identical items follow similar rules. A 3-pack of socks needs to be sealed together with a single FNSKU and a "Sold as set" label.

Create a new ASIN for your bundle. Do not try to list bundles under an existing single-item ASIN. Amazon treats bundles as unique products with their own listing.

## Creating and Managing Shipping Plans

A shipping plan tells Amazon what you are sending and where it is going. Getting this right is essential.

Start by creating your shipping plan in Seller Central. Select the products and quantities you want to send. Amazon will tell you which fulfillment centers to ship to.

In 2025, Amazon's inventory placement service affects where your products go. You can choose to send everything to one warehouse, but you pay an inbound placement fee for this convenience. Or you can split your shipment across multiple warehouses to avoid the fee.

The inbound placement fee ranges from $0.27 to $1.58 per unit depending on product size and weight. For lightweight items, this fee can eat into your margins significantly. Calculate whether the convenience is worth the cost for your specific products.

Box weight must not exceed 50 pounds. Individual boxes over 50 pounds will be rejected. For heavy items, this limits how many units you can put in each box.

Box dimensions should not exceed 25 inches on any side for standard-size products. Oversized items have different limits. Check Amazon's current dimension guidelines before packing.

Label each box with the FBA shipment label Amazon provides. Place it on a flat surface of the box where it will not be folded or obscured by packing tape.

## Inbound Placement Fees Explained

Amazon introduced inbound placement fees to offset the cost of distributing inventory across their network. This is one of the biggest changes affecting FBA sellers in recent years.

When you send all your inventory to a single Amazon warehouse, Amazon has to redistribute it to other fulfillment centers closer to customers. This costs Amazon money. The inbound placement fee passes that cost to sellers.

There are several options for managing this fee. You can let Amazon assign multiple ship-to locations and split your shipment accordingly. This avoids the fee but increases your shipping costs and complexity.

Alternatively, you can use Amazon's partnered carrier program to get discounted shipping rates. This can offset some of the placement fee cost.

The best approach depends on your product size, weight, and margins. Do the math for each product. Some sellers find that paying the placement fee and shipping to one location is cheaper overall. Others save money by splitting shipments.

Keep in mind that inbound placement fees are separate from storage fees. Both add up. Monitor your total FBA cost per unit regularly and adjust your pricing accordingly.

## Choosing the Right FBA Prep Center

Not all prep centers are created equal. Here is what to look for when choosing a partner.

**Location** is the most important factor. A prep center near major shipping hubs reduces transit time to Amazon warehouses. Shorter transit means your inventory gets checked in faster and becomes available for sale sooner.

**Services offered** should match your needs. Some prep centers only handle labeling and bagging. Others offer inspection, bundling, kitting, photography, and custom packaging. Make sure your prep center can handle everything your products require.

**Turnaround time** affects your cash flow. Ask how long it takes from receiving your products to shipping them out to Amazon. Good prep centers complete orders in 1 to 3 business days. Anything longer than 5 business days will slow down your inventory pipeline.

**Pricing transparency** is essential. Get a clear breakdown of costs per unit for each service. Watch out for hidden fees like receiving fees, storage fees, and rush order surcharges.

**Technology integration** makes communication smoother. The best prep centers connect with your Seller Central account and provide real-time status updates. You should be able to see when products arrive, when they are prepped, and when they ship.

**Quality control** keeps your account healthy. Ask about error rates. A good prep center has an error rate below 1 percent. One wrong label can result in inventory going to the wrong ASIN, which can create serious problems for your account.

## FBA Prep Cost Breakdown

Understanding your prep costs per unit is critical for pricing your products correctly.

**Labeling** typically costs $0.20 to $0.50 per unit. This includes printing and applying the FNSKU label and covering existing barcodes.

**Poly bagging** runs $0.25 to $0.75 per unit depending on the bag size and whether a suffocation warning label is needed.

**Bubble wrapping** costs $0.50 to $1.50 per unit depending on the item size and number of wrap layers required.

**Bundling** ranges from $0.50 to $2.00 per bundle depending on the number of items and packaging type.

**Inspection** costs $0.10 to $0.30 per unit for a basic quality check.

**Box and shipping supplies** add $1 to $3 per box depending on size and materials.

Total prep cost per unit typically falls between $0.75 and $3.00 for standard items. Complex products with multiple prep steps cost more. Always factor prep costs into your product sourcing calculations before committing to a new product.

## Common FBA Prep Mistakes

Mistakes in FBA prep cost time and money. Here are the most common errors and how to avoid them.

**Using the wrong FNSKU.** This sends your inventory to the wrong listing. Always double-check FNSKU labels against your shipping plan. A single transposed digit can create a major problem.

**Forgetting suffocation warnings.** This is an easy requirement to overlook, especially when you are prepping hundreds of units. Build it into your standard process so it never gets missed.

**Overweight boxes.** Boxes over 50 pounds get rejected. Weigh every box before sealing it. It sounds tedious, but a rejected shipment is far more inconvenient.

**Poor barcode quality.** Labels that cannot be scanned slow down check-in and may result in your inventory going missing. Invest in a good printer and quality label stock.

**Not covering existing barcodes.** If Amazon's scanner picks up a UPC instead of your FNSKU, your inventory may get commingled with other sellers. This can lead to counterfeit complaints and account issues.

**Ignoring expiration date requirements.** Products with expiration dates need them visible on the outside. If Amazon cannot see the date, they may refuse the shipment or quarantine your inventory.

**Shipping without a confirmed plan.** Never send products to Amazon without a confirmed shipping plan in Seller Central. Unplanned shipments get rejected and may result in penalties.

**Overpacking boxes.** Cramming too many units into one box causes damage. Products arrive crushed or scratched. Leave enough room for products to be protected without being loose.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I do FBA prep myself?**
Yes, but it may not be the best use of your time. Many sellers start by prepping items themselves and switch to a prep center as their volume grows. If you are prepping more than 100 units per week, a prep center is usually more efficient and cost-effective.

**How do I know if my product needs poly bagging?**
Check Amazon's prep requirements in Seller Central. Search for your product's category and read the specific prep guidance. When in doubt, bag it. Over-prepping is better than having a shipment rejected.

**What happens if Amazon rejects my shipment?**
Amazon will notify you of the issue and give you options. You can have the shipment returned to you at your cost, have Amazon dispose of the items, or in some cases, have Amazon fix the issue and charge you a fee. Repeated rejections can lead to shipping plan restrictions.

**How long does it take for inventory to check in at Amazon?**
Typically 3 to 7 business days after delivery. During peak seasons like Q4, check-in can take 2 to 3 weeks. Plan your inventory pipeline accordingly and ship well in advance of high-demand periods.

**Is it worth paying inbound placement fees?**
It depends on your product and margins. Calculate the total cost both ways. Compare split shipments with multiple carrier charges versus a single shipment with the placement fee. For lightweight items, splitting often saves money. For heavy items, the placement fee may be cheaper than multiple shipping charges.

**How often do FBA requirements change?**
Amazon makes small updates throughout the year and larger policy changes once or twice a year. Subscribe to Amazon seller news and join seller forums to stay current. Your prep center should also keep you informed of relevant changes.

**What is the difference between FNSKU and UPC?**
A UPC is a universal product code that identifies the product itself. An FNSKU is an Amazon-specific barcode that identifies the product and the seller. Using FNSKU keeps your inventory separate from other sellers and prevents commingling issues.

## Getting Started with FBA Prep

If you are new to FBA, start with a small test shipment. Prep 20 to 50 units following Amazon's guidelines carefully. Track the process, note any issues, and refine your procedures before scaling up.

As your business grows, partner with a reliable prep center that understands Amazon's requirements inside and out. The right partner saves you time, reduces errors, and keeps your account in good standing.

FBA prep may not be the most exciting part of selling on Amazon. But it is one of the most important. Getting it right means your inventory arrives at Amazon ready to sell. Getting it wrong means delays, fees, and frustrated customers. Invest in doing it right from the start.
