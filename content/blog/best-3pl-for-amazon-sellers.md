Amazon permanently discontinued its own in-house FBA prep and labeling services as of January 1, 2026. That means barcode labeling, poly bagging, bubble wrapping, boxing, and set/bundle creation — all of it — are no longer things Amazon will do for you on inbound, at any price. Every unit has to arrive at an Amazon fulfillment center already 100% compliant. Sellers who used to rely on Amazon's own prep service as a fallback now have to handle it themselves or through a third-party prep provider — there's no third option.

This is a bigger shift than it sounds. It means the choice of prep partner isn't a cost-optimization decision anymore; it's an operational requirement for every FBA seller.

## What Actually Changed on January 1, 2026

Before this change, Amazon offered its own FBA Label Service and prep options at the fulfillment center, and would sometimes prep non-compliant inventory on arrival for a fee — the notorious "prep defect fee" model. That safety net is gone. As of this year:

- Amazon does not apply FNSKU labels for you on inbound.
- Amazon does not poly bag, bubble wrap, or box items on your behalf.
- Amazon does not assemble sets, bundles, or kits.
- Non-compliant inventory isn't fixed and billed anymore — it's refused or returned, which is far more disruptive to your sales timeline than a per-unit fee ever was.

This applies across every inbound method: standard small parcel, AWD (Amazon Warehousing and Distribution), AGL (Amazon Global Logistics), SEND, and shipments created through the Supply Chain Portal.

## Why This Makes Prep Location a Bigger Deal, Not a Smaller One

With Amazon out of the prep business entirely, the round-trip between "prep provider" and "Amazon fulfillment center" matters more than it used to, because there's zero margin for a prep mistake to get caught and fixed downstream anymore. DeliveryGroup's Florence, Kentucky facility sits 5 miles from Amazon's CVG Air Hub, one of Amazon's largest air cargo facilities. In practice that means:

- **Same-day inbound delivery** to Amazon's local receiving docks is possible for many shipments, shrinking the window between "prepped" and "sellable."
- **Lower transportation cost** moving prepped inventory those 5 miles versus shipping cross-country from a prep facility elsewhere.
- **Faster reaction time if Amazon flags something.** Being minutes from the hub means a shipment plan adjustment or a re-prep doesn't cost a week in transit.

## Category-Specific Prep, Now Mandatory Across the Board

Amazon's prep rules were always category-specific; the difference now is there's no fallback if you get them wrong.

- **Standard items** — FNSKU labeling, with poly bagging or bubble wrap depending on packaging fragility.
- **Apparel** — poly bagged with a suffocation warning printed in the required font size and languages, hangers removed or secured, existing barcodes covered.
- **Shoes** — boxed or bagged so the shoe box itself isn't the shipping container, since Amazon treats the shoe box as part of the product.
- **Supplements and health products** — lot tracking and minimum remaining-shelf-life requirements that vary by category; inventory too close to expiration is refused outright, not flagged for review.
- **Oversized and bulky items** — different labeling, palletizing, and sometimes freight-inbound requirements, with fee structures that reward accurate prep the first time.

DeliveryGroup preps to Amazon's current category rules directly, and adjusts automatically as Amazon updates guidelines — which, given how recent the January 2026 change is, is worth confirming with any prep provider you're evaluating right now.

## Merchant Fulfilled and Seller Fulfilled Prime

FBA isn't the only fulfillment model on Amazon, and the January 2026 change doesn't touch merchant-fulfilled orders directly — but it does make a single-inventory-pool setup more valuable, since you're now maintaining prep infrastructure regardless of which channel a given unit ships through.

- **Merchant Fulfilled** orders ship from DeliveryGroup's warehouse directly to the customer, with tracking synced back to Amazon automatically.
- **Seller Fulfilled Prime (SFP)** requires meeting Amazon's own delivery-speed and reliability thresholds to keep the Prime badge on self-fulfilled listings — ground shipping from Florence, KY reaching roughly 80% of the US in 2–3 days makes hitting those thresholds realistic without paying for expedited service on every order.
- **Backup capacity during FBA stockouts** — merchant-fulfilled listings from the same inventory pool keep a product sellable while FBA inventory is replenished, protecting ranking during the gap.

## One Inventory Pool for FBA Prep and Merchant Fulfilled

Keeping FBA-bound and merchant-fulfilled inventory in the same physical pool means you're not pre-committing stock to one channel before you know which one needs it. When a batch needs to go to Amazon, it gets prepped and sent from the same warehouse floor that's filling merchant orders same-day. That flexibility matters more now than it used to: since prep work has to happen somewhere for every single FBA-bound unit, having your merchant-fulfillment and FBA-prep operations under one roof means you're not paying to duplicate that infrastructure across two providers.

## Reducing Amazon Inbound Placement Fees

Amazon's inbound placement fee structure rewards sellers who ship efficiently-split shipments to the fulfillment centers Amazon's own algorithm recommends, rather than concentrating everything at one location. A prep provider close to a major hub has more flexibility to route inventory the way Amazon's placement system prefers, without absorbing large internal transportation costs themselves. DeliveryGroup can advise on shipment timing and splits based on what's currently working best in the network.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Amazon really not do any FBA prep anymore?

Correct, as of January 1, 2026, Amazon discontinued all in-house FBA prep and labeling services in the US — no labeling, poly bagging, bubble wrapping, boxing, or bundling, and non-compliant inventory is now refused rather than prepped and billed.

### Does this affect every inbound shipping method?

Yes — standard small parcel, AWD, AGL, SEND, and Supply Chain Portal shipments are all subject to the same 100%-compliant-on-arrival requirement.

### How fast can DeliveryGroup turn around FBA prep?

Most shipments are prepped within one to two business days, with same-day options available for urgent shipments given the 5-mile proximity to Amazon's CVG Air Hub.

### Can I keep one inventory pool for both FBA and merchant-fulfilled orders?

Yes. Inventory sits in one place at the Florence, KY warehouse, and DeliveryGroup preps and ships to FBA or picks and ships merchant orders from the same pool as needed.

### Does DeliveryGroup handle Seller Fulfilled Prime volume?

Yes — the 2–3 day ground coverage to roughly 80% of the US from the Florence, KY location is built to support SFP's delivery-speed requirements without relying on express shipping for every order.

### What happens if my inventory doesn't meet Amazon's prep requirements now?

Since Amazon no longer preps or fixes non-compliant inventory on arrival, it's refused or returned rather than corrected and billed — which makes getting prep right before it ships significantly more important than it was under the old system.
