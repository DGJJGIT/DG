## What Is FBA Prep and Why Does It Matter?

FBA stands for Fulfillment by Amazon. When you use FBA, you send your products to Amazon's warehouses. Amazon stores them, picks and packs orders, and ships them to customers. They also handle returns and customer service for those orders.

But before Amazon will accept your products, they have to be prepped according to very specific guidelines. FBA prep is the process of getting your products ready to meet those requirements.

FBA prep can include:

- Applying FNSKU labels (Amazon's unique barcode for each product)
- Poly bagging items that need protection
- Bubble wrapping fragile products
- Adding suffocation warnings to poly bags
- Bundling multi-pack items together
- Boxing oversized or oddly shaped products
- Removing or covering existing barcodes
- Adding "sold as set" stickers to bundled items
- Shrink wrapping products that need it

Every product category has its own rules. Shoes need to be bagged differently than supplements. Apparel has different requirements than electronics. Getting any of these steps wrong can cause Amazon to reject your shipment or charge you extra fees.

## The Real Cost of FBA Prep Defects

Amazon does not just reject improperly prepped shipments. They charge you for fixing them. These are called prep defect fees, and they add up fast.

If your product arrives at an Amazon fulfillment center without the right label, Amazon will apply it for you and charge a fee per unit. If items are not properly bagged, Amazon charges for that too. If products are not bundled correctly, you guessed it, more fees.

These fees typically range from a few cents to over a dollar per unit. That might not sound like much, but if you are sending thousands of units, the costs pile up quickly. Sending 5,000 units with a labeling defect could cost you hundreds or even thousands of dollars in fees.

And the financial hit is not the worst part. Repeated violations can lead to warnings, restrictions, or a coaching from Amazon. In serious cases, your ability to send inventory to FBA can be suspended. That means your products go offline, your sales stop, and your rankings drop.

A good FBA prep service gets it right the first time, every time. That saves you money and keeps your account in good standing.

## FNSKU Labeling Done Right

Every product you send to Amazon FBA needs an FNSKU label. FNSKU stands for Fulfillment Network Stock Keeping Unit. It is a barcode that Amazon uses to identify your specific product in their warehouse.

This label is different from a UPC or EAN barcode. The FNSKU is unique to your seller account. It tells Amazon that the product belongs to you and should be tracked under your inventory. This prevents your product from getting mixed up with other sellers' inventory, which is called commingling.

Applying FNSKU labels sounds simple, but it has to be done correctly:

- The label must be placed on the right spot on the product or packaging.
- It must be scannable by Amazon's warehouse equipment.
- It must not cover any important product information like ingredients or warnings.
- Any existing barcodes must be covered so Amazon's scanners do not get confused.
- The label must be applied flat, without wrinkles or bubbles that could make it unscannable.

## Poly Bagging and Product Protection

Many products need to be placed in clear poly bags before going to Amazon. This includes items with loose parts, products that could be damaged by dust or moisture, and items in packaging that is not sealed.

Amazon requires that poly bags over a certain size include a suffocation warning. The warning must be printed on the bag in a specific font size and in multiple languages. Missing this step is one of the most common reasons for prep defect fees.

Poly bagging requirements also vary by product type:

- **Apparel** must be bagged to protect from dust and moisture during storage.
- **Plush items** and soft goods need bags to prevent them from getting dirty.
- **Items with multiple pieces** need bags to keep everything together.
- **Products with exposed surfaces** that could get scratched or scuffed need protection.

## Multi-Category FBA Prep

Amazon has different prep requirements for different product categories. What works for a standard-sized item does not work for apparel, shoes, supplements, or oversized products. DeliveryGroup handles all of these categories.

### Standard Products

These are your typical boxed or packaged items. They need FNSKU labels, and some may need poly bagging or bubble wrap depending on the packaging. Most standard products are straightforward to prep, but the details still matter.

### Apparel

Clothing items need to be poly bagged with suffocation warnings. Hangers may need to be removed or secured. Labels must be visible through the bag or applied to the outside. Amazon is strict about apparel prep because loose items can cause problems in their automated fulfillment centers.

### Shoes

Footwear must be bagged or boxed so that the shoe box is not the shipping container. Amazon considers the shoe box part of the product, so it needs to be protected during transit. Each pair needs to be in a poly bag or placed inside an outer box.

### Supplements and Health Products

These items have additional requirements around expiration dates and lot tracking. Labels must include specific information, and products close to their expiration date may not be accepted by Amazon. Amazon requires at least a certain number of remaining shelf life days, and that number varies by category.

### Oversized Products

Large and heavy items have different labeling and packaging requirements. They may need special palletizing or freight shipping to Amazon. Oversized products also have different fee structures, so accurate prep can help you avoid unnecessary charges.

DeliveryGroup's team knows the rules for each category. They stay up to date on Amazon's changing requirements so you do not have to. When Amazon updates its guidelines, the team adjusts their process accordingly.

## Reducing Amazon Placement Fees

Amazon introduced inbound placement fees that apply when you send inventory to a single Amazon fulfillment center instead of spreading it across multiple locations. The fees vary by product size and weight, but they can be significant for large shipments.

There are a few strategies to reduce placement fees:

- **Amazon-optimized shipment plans:** Working with Amazon's recommended shipment splits to distribute inventory more efficiently across their network.
- **Strategic prep location:** Shipping from a location near a major Amazon hub can reduce the distance Amazon needs to move your inventory internally, which can affect fee calculations.
- **Timing your shipments:** Sending inventory during less busy periods can sometimes result in better placement options from Amazon.
- **Partial shipments:** Instead of sending all your inventory at once, breaking it into smaller shipments can sometimes result in more favorable placement.
