Amazon Business runs on the same fulfillment infrastructure as consumer Amazon (FBA or merchant fulfilled), but the order requirements underneath it are genuinely different — starting with the fact that every Amazon Business order needs a compliant, itemized invoice, something consumer Amazon orders never require at all.

## The Invoice Requirement Most Sellers Underestimate

Amazon Business requires sellers to provide a proper invoice for every B2B order — itemized, with applicable tax detail — available for the business buyer to download from their account. This isn't optional paperwork; business buyers frequently need it for their own accounting and procurement records, and missing or non-compliant invoices show up as a real friction point that can affect your standing on the channel. A fulfillment setup built only around consumer orders, where no invoice is generated at all, needs an explicit process added for this — it doesn't happen automatically just because the order came through Amazon.

## Tax Exemption Changes What "Correct" Looks Like Per Order

Many Amazon Business buyers purchase through the Amazon Tax Exemption Program (ATEP), meaning specific orders should be invoiced without sales tax applied where the buyer has valid exemption documentation on file. Getting this wrong in either direction — charging tax on an exempt purchase, or failing to document an exemption properly — creates cleanup work for both the buyer and the seller. Order handling needs to respect each order's actual tax-exemption status individually, not apply one blanket rule across all Amazon Business volume.

## Quantity Discounts and Business Pricing Change Order Profiles

Amazon Business supports quantity-based pricing tiers and business-only pricing separate from consumer listings, which means B2B orders on the same ASIN often arrive in meaningfully larger quantities than a typical consumer purchase — a business buyer ordering a case-pack quantity of office supplies looks nothing like an individual buying one unit. Fulfillment needs to handle that quantity variance without treating every order as a single-unit consumer pick, including case-pack or multi-unit packaging appropriate to bulk business orders rather than individual retail presentation.

## One Inventory Pool for Consumer and Business Orders

Since Amazon Business and consumer Amazon orders both draw from the same ASIN and, typically, the same FBA or merchant-fulfilled inventory, keeping one inventory pool across both channels avoids the capital inefficiency of splitting stock artificially between "consumer" and "business" allocations that don't actually need to be separate. DeliveryGroup fulfills both from the same Florence, KY facility and inventory pool, applying the invoicing and tax-exemption handling automatically based on whether a given order came through the Amazon Business channel.

## FBA Prep Still Applies — With the Same 2026 Change

Amazon Business orders fulfilled via FBA are subject to the same prep requirements as any FBA inventory, including Amazon's January 1, 2026 discontinuation of in-house prep and labeling services — meaning FNSKU labeling, poly bagging, and bundling all need to arrive 100% compliant regardless of whether the eventual buyer is a consumer or a business account. For sellers running both channels through FBA, this is a shared requirement, not a separate one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do you generate the itemized invoices Amazon Business requires?

Yes — compliant, itemized invoices are generated for B2B orders as part of the fulfillment workflow, since Amazon Business requires them on every order and consumer orders don't.

### How is tax-exemption status (ATEP) handled per order?

Each order's tax-exemption status is respected individually based on the buyer's documentation on file, rather than applying a single rule across all Amazon Business volume.

### Can you handle bulk or case-pack quantity orders differently from single-unit consumer orders?

Yes — business orders placed at quantity-discount tiers are packaged appropriately for bulk shipment rather than individual retail presentation.

### Do consumer and Amazon Business orders draw from the same inventory?

Yes, one inventory pool serves both, avoiding the inefficiency of artificially splitting stock between channels that don't need to be separate.

### Does the January 2026 FBA prep change apply to Amazon Business orders too?

Yes — any FBA-bound inventory needs to arrive 100% compliant regardless of the eventual buyer, since Amazon no longer preps or fixes non-compliant inventory on arrival for any channel.
