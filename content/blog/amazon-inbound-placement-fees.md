## What Are Amazon Inbound Placement Fees?

In March 2024, Amazon introduced inbound placement fees for FBA sellers. These fees changed how sellers think about sending inventory to Amazon fulfillment centers. Before this change, Amazon distributed your inventory across their network for free. Now they charge for it.

Here is how it works. When you create an FBA shipment, Amazon assigns your inventory to one or more fulfillment centers. If you send everything to a single location and let Amazon redistribute it, you pay an inbound placement fee. If you split your shipment across multiple fulfillment centers yourself, the fee is reduced or eliminated.

Amazon frames this as a way to reduce their internal transfer costs. For sellers, it is an additional cost that can significantly impact margins, especially on low-priced or heavy items.

## Understanding the Fee Structure

The inbound placement fee varies based on product size, weight, and how many locations you ship to. Amazon offers three inbound shipping options.

**Amazon-optimized splits (minimal placement fee).** Amazon tells you exactly which fulfillment centers to send inventory to. You split your shipment accordingly. This option has the lowest placement fee because you are doing the distribution work yourself. For standard-size items, this typically means shipping to 4 or more fulfillment centers.

**Partial splits.** You send inventory to fewer locations than Amazon's optimized recommendation but more than one. The placement fee is reduced compared to sending everything to one location but higher than the fully optimized option.

**Single location (highest placement fee).** You send everything to one fulfillment center and let Amazon redistribute internally. This is the simplest option operationally but carries the highest placement fee.

For standard-size items, the inbound placement fee for single-location shipments ranges from $0.21 to $1.58 per unit depending on size and weight. For large bulky items, fees can reach $6.23 per unit or more. These fees add up fast when you are sending thousands of units.

## The Amazon Warehousing and Distribution (AWD) Program

Amazon Warehousing and Distribution, or AWD, is another tool for managing inbound placement costs. AWD is Amazon's upstream storage program that feeds inventory into FBA fulfillment centers.

Here is how AWD works. You send bulk inventory to an AWD facility. Amazon stores it in their warehouse. When your FBA inventory levels drop, Amazon automatically replenishes from your AWD stock. The replenishment goes directly from AWD to FBA fulfillment centers, and Amazon handles the distribution.

**The key benefit.** Inventory sent through AWD is exempt from inbound placement fees. Amazon absorbs the distribution cost because inventory is already within their network. For sellers who use AWD, this can completely eliminate placement fees.

**AWD costs.** AWD charges storage fees that vary by time of year and product size. Base storage rates are competitive with third-party warehousing, but long-term storage fees can add up. You need to weigh AWD storage costs against the placement fees you would otherwise pay.

**AWD limitations.** You lose some control over inventory timing with AWD. Replenishment happens on Amazon's schedule. If you need inventory at FBA centers by a specific date, AWD may not be fast enough. Also, AWD facilities have specific receiving requirements that add prep complexity.

**Best use case.** AWD works well for steady-selling products where you can maintain a buffer stock. It is less ideal for seasonal products or new launches where you need precise control over inventory timing.

## Calculating Your Fee Impact

Before you can optimize, you need to understand your current cost. Here is how to calculate the impact of inbound placement fees on your business.

**Pull your placement fee data.** In Seller Central, go to your FBA fee reports. Amazon breaks down inbound placement fees by shipment. Export this data for the last 3-6 months.

**Calculate fee per unit.** Divide your total placement fees by the number of units shipped. This gives you your average placement fee per unit.

**Calculate fee as a percentage of revenue.** Divide your total placement fees by your total revenue for the same period. For most sellers, placement fees represent 1-5% of revenue. For sellers with heavy or bulky products, it can be higher.

**Model scenarios.** Calculate what your fees would be under each shipping option. What would you pay with Amazon-optimized splits? What would you pay with single-location shipments? The difference shows you the optimization opportunity.

**Factor in freight costs.** Lower placement fees require shipping to more locations. That means higher freight costs. The optimal strategy minimizes the combined cost of placement fees plus freight. A centrally located prep center like Delivery Group reduces the freight side of this equation.

## Ten Tips to Minimize Inbound Placement Fees

Here are specific, actionable strategies to reduce your placement fee burden.

**1. Always choose Amazon-optimized splits.** Unless the freight cost to multiple locations exceeds the placement fee savings, the optimized split option is almost always the best choice.

**2. Use a centrally located prep center.** Central locations reduce freight costs to multiple FCs, making the multi-split strategy more economical. Northern Kentucky is ideal.

**3. Consolidate shipments.** Larger, less frequent shipments to each FC are cheaper per unit in freight than small, frequent ones. Plan your replenishment cycles to batch inventory sends.

**4. Evaluate AWD for steady sellers.** If you have products with consistent demand, AWD can eliminate placement fees entirely. Run the numbers comparing AWD storage costs to placement fees.

**5. Optimize product packaging.** Smaller, lighter products incur lower placement fees. If you can reduce your product dimensions or weight without compromising quality, your fees drop.

**6. Time your shipments strategically.** Avoid sending large shipments during peak season when fees may be higher and FC assignments may be less favorable. Pre-position inventory before peak when possible.

**7. Monitor fee changes.** Amazon adjusts fee structures regularly. Stay current on fee updates and adjust your strategy accordingly.

**8. Use Amazon's fee calculator.** Before creating a shipment, use Amazon's fee preview tools to see estimated placement fees for different shipping options. Compare before committing.

**9. Build placement fees into your pricing.** If you cannot eliminate the fees, account for them in your product pricing and margin calculations. Many sellers undercount their true FBA costs.

**10. Partner with an experienced prep provider.** FBA prep providers handle multi-FC distribution daily. Their expertise and location advantages can reduce your total inbound cost below what you could achieve on your own.
