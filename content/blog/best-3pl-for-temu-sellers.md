Temu's US "Local" seller program is a different animal from the cross-border dropship model most people associate with the platform. Local sellers ship domestically-held inventory to US customers directly, competing on Temu's price expectations without the multi-week cross-border transit time — which means the whole model depends on a fulfillment operation that can hit tight handling windows on products with very little price room to absorb mistakes.

## Temu's Handling-Time SLA Is the Real Constraint

Temu enforces strict handling-time requirements for Local sellers — commonly a short window between order placement and carrier scan — tracked as a seller performance metric that affects account standing and search placement inside the app. Miss it repeatedly and listings lose visibility even when the product itself is fine. That makes handling time a warehouse-operations problem as much as a shipping-speed one: DeliveryGroup's same-day pick-and-pack for orders received before the daily cutoff is built around exactly this kind of SLA-sensitive marketplace, not a generic next-day promise.

## Thin Margins Make Per-Unit Shipping Cost Existential

Temu's pricing model leaves very little room per order to absorb shipping cost. A few cents in avoidable packaging waste or a non-optimized carrier choice is the difference between a profitable SKU and a break-even one. Two things matter most:

- **Rate access at volume.** DeliveryGroup's DHL partnership, anchored by the CVG Super Hub sitting 5 miles from the Florence, KY warehouse, gets Temu sellers rate tiers that are hard to negotiate independently at lower per-seller volumes.
- **Packaging discipline.** Poly mailers instead of boxes wherever the product allows it, right-sized packaging instead of one-size-fits-all, and no unnecessary dunnage — all of which sound minor until you're shipping thousands of low-ASP units a month and every gram of DIM weight is margin.

## Compliance Documentation Isn't Optional

Temu requires country-of-origin declarations and product safety documentation for many categories, particularly textiles, electronics, and anything intended for children. A 3PL that treats this as the seller's problem alone creates real risk: incomplete documentation can hold a shipment or trigger a compliance review that stalls an entire SKU's sales. DeliveryGroup tracks compliance documentation per SKU alongside standard receiving, so it travels with the inventory instead of living in a separate spreadsheet someone forgets to check before a shipment goes out.

## High SKU Count, Low Per-Unit Value

Temu sellers competing on price commonly run wide catalogs with many low-cost SKUs rather than a few higher-margin ones. That's a warehouse-organization problem more than a shipping one: accurate bin-level tracking across hundreds of SKUs, fast pick paths, and inventory counts that don't drift — a stockout on a $4 item is just as disruptive to search ranking as one on a $40 item, with far less margin to justify fixing it after the fact.
